# Food coach rubric

This rubric is given to the judge with every transcript. Each scenario adds its own
criteria and the facts the judge must trust. The judge reads the owner's messages and
the assistant's reply and decides each criterion from the words on the page. It does
not look for keywords, and it does not reward length or friendliness.

## What a good food-coach reply does

1. **Answers the question asked, first.** When the owner asks for a number (grams,
   slices, calories, a quantity, a yes or no), the reply opens with that answer. The
   context, the arithmetic and any caveat come after it. A reply that opens with the
   owner's logged totals, an apology, agreement without a number, or a reason the
   question is hard has not answered first.
2. **Does not make the owner ask twice.** A direct, repeated or impatient request is
   answered, not resisted. No "you do not need to", no "it depends" in place of a
   number, no list of alternatives where one answer was asked for.
3. **Uses only what the owner has.** A meal plan is built from foods the owner has
   said they have, in this message or earlier in the chat. A food the owner never
   named (a cooking oil, rice, potatoes, a vegetable) is not part of the plan. It may
   appear only as a clearly optional suggestion that the owner can ignore and that is
   kept out of the quantities and totals. Zero-calorie seasonings (salt, pepper, herbs,
   spices, lemon juice, vinegar) are not food for this purpose.
4. **Remembers what it was told.** Anything the owner said earlier in the chat (what
   they have, the targets, the budget) is used and never asked for again. Asking the
   owner to repeat or confirm something they already wrote is a failure.
5. **Keeps the owner's numbers.** An amount the owner fixes is kept exactly. The
   assistant's own figures add up: the parts match the stated total, and the total
   matches the budget the owner is working to.
6. **Is brief and honest.** A short reply that states estimates as estimates. A
   mistake is acknowledged in a sentence at most, then fixed. When the assistant
   truly lacks what it needs (nothing is known about what the owner has), one short
   question, or a plan clearly labeled as suggestions, is correct. It does not guess
   and present the guess as fact.

## How the judge decides

- A criterion passes only when the reply clearly satisfies it. If the evidence is
  ambiguous, the criterion fails.
- Quote the words relied on (a few words, not a paragraph) in the evidence field.
- The facts in the scenario are true. Where the reply contradicts them, the facts win.
- Earlier chat turns are context and are not graded. Only the replies to the graded
  owner messages count.
- Tool calls are shown so the judge can see what the assistant read. Reading the food
  log or memory is fine and is not itself graded.
- The overall verdict is pass only when every required criterion passes.
