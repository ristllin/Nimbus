# Bench link, card and send-path timing (lane RB-M2, 18 Nov 2026)

The rebuild PRD fixes its latency ceilings and its card gates from bench numbers (section 2, "Latency model per
request" and the transcript gates; PLAN.json `measurements_needed_first`). This page holds those numbers, measured on
the bench board, and what the 4.5 s send path and the 3.6 s arrival overhead seen on Lumi consist of.

- Board: unit F1 (Freenove CYD, MAC 14:C1:9F:D2:2A:D0), test-cyd image of lane RB-M2 at 5c712a89, same Wi-Fi as Lumi.
- Holds: 08:33 to 08:42Z and 08:43 to 08:52Z, 18 Nov. Raw logs: `program-2026-10-18/lanes/RB-M2/evidence/bench/hold1`
  and `hold2`. Data: `tests/evals/measured/link.json` (regenerate with
  `python3 tests/hil/test_link_budget.py analyze <out.json> <hold dir>...`).
- Every figure is the device's own clock: the LINKX and CARDTIME console answers time each phase in device ms, and with
  LOGMS on every serial log line carries the device's uptime. No Mac wall clock enters a figure.
- Each row has at least 6 samples (the 120 KB row 100). Median, then min to max.

## 1. The link

One exchange through `agent::deviceTransport()` (the turn's own transport: TLS slot, 4 KB slices, pacing), on
claude-haiku-5-5 with max_tokens 8 so decode stays out of it. Phases: conn = TLS handshake; up = the body's slices
written to the socket; wait = last byte written to the status line (the tail of the upload in flight, the provider's
prefill and first token); total = slot taken to socket closed.

| Body | n | conn ms | up ms | upload B/s | wait ms | total ms |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 60,000 B | 6 | 1,229 (1,224-1,231) | 251 (242-269) | 239,230 | 373 (324-1,113) | 1,870 (1,804-2,592) |
| 85,000 B | 6 | 1,225 | 343 (316-357) | 248,180 | 428 | 2,016 (1,931-2,674) |
| 120,000 B | 6 | 1,227 | 494 (474-531) | 242,919 | 416 | 2,162 (2,114-2,297) |
| 120,000 B | 100 | 1,228 | 493 (434-607) | 243,655 | 434 | 2,192 (2,018-3,976), p90 2,954 |
| 180,000 B | 6 | 1,228 | 747 (685-779) | 241,173 | 1,333 (394-1,388) | 3,291 (2,408-3,359) |
| 250,000 B | 6 | 1,230 | 1,032 (1,003-1,106) | 242,330 | 468 (373-1,378) | 2,731 (2,712-3,663) |

- 100 requests at 120 KB: 100 answered 200, **no timeout**, slowest 3.98 s against the 120 s read window.
- Upload rate, least squares over 130 exchanges: **238,600 B/s** marginal, intercept about 0 ms. No pacing fired
  (paced = 0 on every exchange). The rate is the socket write; the last few KB still in flight land in wait.
- The wait is bimodal (about 0.4 s or 1.35 s) at every size: the provider's queue, not the body.
- Handshake only (a bodiless GET): api.anthropic.com 1,523 ms (1,282-1,531), api.openai.com 1,242 ms (1,231-1,438),
  api.telegram.org 399 ms (362-439, n=12).
- The connect to Anthropic is bimodal: about 1.23 s on every padded request, the Files upload and the 2-photo album
  (n=142), and about 1.53 s on the bodiless GETs and the 4-photo album (n=12). conn counts from the slot to the end of
  the handshake (client setup, any connect retry and the TLS handshake).

The latency model's constants (PRD section 2: 1.5 s connect + body / 70,000 B per s):

| Body | PRD model, link part | Measured conn + up |
|---|---|---|
| 60 KB | 2.36 s | 1.48 s |
| 85 KB | 2.71 s | 1.57 s |
| 120 KB | 3.21 s | 1.72 s |
| 250 KB | 5.07 s | 2.26 s |

So the link part is **1.23 s connect + body / 238,600 B per s** (1.53 s in the slower connect mode): the connect is
at or under the PRD's 1.5 s, the upload is 3.4 times faster than it assumed. On the bench the Sonnet head's own requests in the turns below (63 to 76 KB,
cached prefix) took 3.70 s (3.34-3.99) from slot to close, upload 0.30 s.

## 2. Photos

Synthetic 800 x 600 JPEGs of about 76.5 KB each (102,000 B of base64), uploaded to the card through the file route.

| What | n | conn ms | up ms | wait ms | total ms |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Files API upload of one photo (76,742 B multipart) | 6 | 1,229 | 319 (299-357) | 473 (418-756) | **2,006** (2,004-2,312) |
| its deletion | 6 | 1,229 | - | 502 (397-1,111) | 1,737 (1,635-2,351) |
| inline album of 2 + 60 KB text (264,662 B) | 6 | 1,224 | 1,079 (1,054-1,155) | 1,046 (512-1,296) | **3,342** (2,821-3,701) |
| inline album of 4 + 60 KB text (468,714 B) | 6 | 1,528 | 2,006 (1,854-2,135) | 1,218 (998-1,365) | **4,712** (4,518-4,951) |

- The inline follow-up (the PRD's 2 newest photos at 200,000 B of base64, base64 spliced at send time as an inline
  head sends them) takes at most 3.7 s: 3.1% of Anthropic's 120 s read window and 4.1% of the routed 90 s window.
  An album of four inline takes at most 5.0 s (4.1% and 5.5%). The inline photo allowance is not bounded by the link.
- The album was measured on an Anthropic inline request (no file references), which is the inline-head wire path;
  no Z.ai or routed run was needed or made (ruling 12).

## 3. The card

CARDTIME on the card lock (`agent::memory::LoopLock`, the lock every gated append takes). wait = ms to take the lock
(idle bench, so 0 throughout), hold = ms held. n = 8 each.

| Operation | Hold median | Range | PRD gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| append 1 KB | 6 ms | 6-58 (first append of the run opens the folder) | 150 ms |
| append 4 KB | 10 ms | 9-12 | 150 ms |
| append 16 KB | 27.5 ms | 26-39 | 150 ms |
| 130 KB sequential read, 8 KB slices (17 holds) | 82.5 ms total, 6 ms longest slice | 82-89 total | restart fill 3 s |
| 20 KB whole-file replace with index write (`files::saveText`) | **131 ms** | 126-**162** | **150 ms** |
| the same 20 KB, part file and rename only | 42 ms | 39-52 | |

- A logged meal (5 transcript appends of 1 to 4 KB) costs about 30 to 50 ms of card time.
- The restart fill (130 KB) is 89 ms at worst, 34 times under its 3 s gate.
- **The whole-file replace through the file store is at the gate**: 2 of 8 runs held the lock 155 and 162 ms. The file
  store's own work beyond writing and renaming the bytes is 89 ms (131 - 42): its room check, the 20 KB content hash,
  the index rewrite (the bench's store holds about 70 files) and one log line. Those were not timed apart. A ledger file
  swap or the legacy mirror's one replace per turn that runs through `files::saveText` sits at the 150 ms gate on its
  own; RB-L1b and RB-I3 should time their own write paths against it before adding anything inside the same hold.

## 4. The send path: model result to Telegram send

11 owner turns on the bench bot (the dev test bot, a settle page through the full receive path: archive, settle hold,
batcher, one turn), head anthropic claude-sonnet-5, short text replies, no tools. From the end of the head's last
exchange (its heaptrace at= + ms=) to "telegram: send ok":

**Bench: 2,527 ms median (1,880-4,265, p90 3,374). Lumi: 4.5 s median.**

Each gap is named by the log line it ends on; the work that ran in it is what that line reports.

| Gap ending at | Median ms | Range | What runs |
|---|---|---|---|
| claims / fabric-loop / fabric-spend | 25 | 20-30 | the response's bookkeeping |
| orchestrator: turn -> | 133 | 120-156 | the head reply's JSON handling and log |
| turn spend: ok | **1,403** | 855-1,819 | engine-1 post-reply work: `applyTurn` (reply enqueued, history rows and memory writes to the card, guards), the open-question observer, then `meterSpend` (usage to the card) |
| context / turn end | 39 | 34-148 | the turn's context line, turn end |
| fold: chatsum.txt saved | **280** | 179-**1,679** | the chat summary file rewritten on the card (its own line gives the save time) |
| telegram: settled page ran | 11 | 9-16 | the settle page's bookkeeping |
| telegram: send ok | **612** | 580-958 | the reply queue's send: a new TLS session to api.telegram.org (handshake 399 ms measured) plus the POST and its answer |

The reply is ready about 0.16 s after the model's result. It then waits about 1.7 s for engine-1's post-reply work and
the summary save, and the send itself costs 0.6 s, two thirds of it a fresh handshake. On Lumi the same blocks are
larger (a bigger store, more history rows, more memory), which is the gap from 2.5 to 4.5 s; that split was not
measured on Lumi (production is not touched).

## 5. The arrival overhead

Arrival is the moment the page comes in (on the bench, the settle hold's first line; on Lumi, the archive line of a
polled page, which is written at receipt). Arrival to turn start: **8,772 ms** median (8,604-9,574), of which the settle
hold is 6.04 to 6.31 s. From the hold's release to "orchestrator: turn host=": **2,624 ms** median (2,494-3,334).
Lumi: 3.6 s over the hold.

| Gap ending at | Median ms | Range | What runs |
|---|---|---|---|
| settle dispatch / msg from | 9 | 6-24 | the batcher hands the message to the turn |
| budget: (history window) | **2,505** | 2,380-3,230 | the pre-turn recall (an embedding request to api.openai.com on a fresh TLS session: the handshake alone is 1,242 ms; the bench board holds 43 vectors), the history read (17 card reads, card lock about 340 ms per the turn's context line) and the prompt compose |
| tool-loop ON / turn host= | 108 | 98-160 | tool list and loop setup |

Inside the hold (not overhead): the typing indicator (one Telegram session) and the archive write (about 0.37 s) both
run while the hold waits.

## 6. What lane RB-TS1 can cut

| Component | Today on the bench | Cut | How |
|---|---|---|---|
| Send waits for post-reply work | 1.40 s + 0.28 s (up to 1.7 s) | about 1.6 s | send the reply as soon as it is final, then run apply's writes, metering and the summary save |
| Telegram handshake per send | 0.40 s | about 0.4 s | keep the Telegram session or open it during the head request's wait (one TLS slot: only between exchanges) |
| Pre-turn recall embedding | about 1.2 s handshake plus the request | most of 1.5 to 2 s | engine 2 drops the pre-turn recall (recall is a tool); on engine 1, skip it on plain chat or reuse the session |
| History card reads | about 0.34 s of lock | some | engine 2 reads its RAM transcript, not 17 card reads |

With the first two cuts the send path is about 0.7 s (target 2.0 s). The arrival target (1.0 s over the hold) needs
the recall request gone; that is engine 2's shape, and on engine 1 it is the one large item RB-TS1 would have to move.
The parts of the 1.4 s post-reply block were not split further: the block is engine-1 code in files this lane does not
own (engine.cpp is a hot file) and engine 2 replaces it.

## 7. What the latency model should read

- Per request: **1.23 s connect (1.53 s in the slow mode) + body / 238,600 B per s** + prefill + about 1.0 s + decode
  (was 1.5 s + body / 70,000). At 85 KB the link part is 1.57 s (1.87 s), not 2.71 s.
- Send path: 2.5 s bench, 4.5 s Lumi today; about 0.7 s if RB-TS1 lands the two send cuts.
- Arrival over the hold: 2.6 s bench, 3.6 s Lumi today; about 1.0 s only without the pre-turn recall request.
- Files upload of one photo: 2.0 s (a new TLS session each; 0.8 s of it after the handshake).
- Inline album follow-up: under 5 s for four photos, far inside every read window.

## How to re-run

Under the unit F1 lock, after a MAC-verified app flash of a test-cyd image:

```
python3 tests/hil/test_link_budget.py bench turns,link,store,photos,hosts,hundred,cleanup <outdir> 26
python3 tests/hil/test_link_budget.py analyze tests/evals/measured/link.json <outdir>...
```

The driver opens /dev/cu.usbmodem1101 only, sets sound 0 and voice replies off first and reads them back, restores the
head and Telegram settings after the turns, and never prints a key (the bot token and chat id are loaded inside the
process and redacted in every log line). Host tests: `python3 -m pytest tests/hil/test_link_budget.py
tests/hil/test_store_timing.py`.
