# Request budgets

Generated by `test_request_budget` from the request budget code (`lib/core/src/orch_request_budget.cpp`,
`deriveRequestContext` in `lib/core/src/orch_budget.cpp`) and the load-aware output caps
(`lib/harness/src/providers/output_caps.cpp`). Do not edit by hand: re-run
`GOLDEN_UPDATE=1 pio test -e native -f test_request_budget` and read the diff.

Each head's budget comes out of one formula: its window (the provider's listed window, else the
family table), the bytes per token of its wire (rNom nominal, rSafe dense), the read window of its host,
and the slowest measured prefill and decode rates. Bytes is the body the head may send: the minimum of
100,000 tokens, the latency bound, 0.75 of the window and 0.9 of a price tier, never under the floor of
85,000 B (or 0.85 of a tiny window). Bound names which one decided. Target is bytes over rNom, in tokens.
Read window is in seconds, prefill and decode in tokens per second (decode at the target's context),
and every other figure in bytes; the history marks are given in tokens first (at the head's rNom, owner
ruling 82), then in bytes. No figure is below the head's v5.1.2 value. The history marks (Hist
max, soft, keep, Fold step, Rows, Trigger) are shown under the default history profile, full: each
head's own marks, as v5.1.2 derives them (smaller profiles are the owner's choice: History profiles
below). A split-prompt head at the 85,000 B floor sizes its history from the measured
overhead of the old engine's request (see Floor heads below); any other head at the floor keeps the
v5.1.1 history window and its 48 KB fold trigger. Caps are the first attempt and the retry of a
final round at the budget as the wire sends them (a routed round counts its second upload, and a first
attempt never goes under the host's minimum final), on a healthy and on a fragmented heap (none: the
host sends no output cap).

Replay changed meaning in v5.2.0 (lane RB-BGb, ruling 93 item 3). It was the most of a turn's own tool
traffic (calls, results, prose) a request carried before older rounds folded (about 15,000 tokens,
37,500 B, on claude-sonnet-5-5). It is now the least: a request carries the turn's tool traffic up to
what its body has free after the prompt, the history and the summary, and older rounds fold only when
the request would otherwise not fit, each named with its results.get tag. The history max still
reserves this figure for the turn's tool traffic.

## Floor heads

A head whose turn is a split prompt (the history as messages) at the 85,000 B floor, a routed head
above all, sizes its history from the measured overhead of the old engine's request: the system
blocks, the turn's input message, the tools array and the envelope of one owner turn of the synthetic
busy twin, which test_floor_dialogue measures on the real build (62912 B on the
Anthropic wire, 64832 B on OpenAI's), plus the summary at its cap, a 512 B margin and 2048 B for one small tool round. What is
left is the window's high water in window bytes (the wire spends 120 and 130 B for every 100 B of
window on the 17 Nov shape). A shift lands at 4/5 of it and the fold that follows keeps 3/4, so the
request carries between 3/4 and all of the room (v5.1.1's window fell to half at a shift); the fold
trigger stays v5.1.1's 48 KB. Where that leaves no more than v5.1.1's window, the head keeps v5.1.1's
window exactly (the floor branch), and so does a head on a wire whose overhead is not measured
(Mistral, Z.ai and the other router upstreams).

## The spending advice

Nightly at the device's day start (lib/core `budget_governor.h`; nimbusd runs the same rule). Nothing
changes the profile by itself (owner ruling 81):

1. It projects the month: the spend so far plus the mean of the last seven observed nights (at least
   three, all under the profile in force) times the days left.
2. Over 0.9 of the ceiling it tells the owner, once that month (Telegram, the web chat, the Usage
   page's record, the durable log): the projection, the date the ceiling is reached at that rate,
   and the largest smaller profile that would fit, by each profile's measured cost ratio (tiny 578, small 793,
   medium 1000, large 1448 thousandths, from tests/evals/measured/cost.json; full is counted at large's
   ratio), or that none would. The owner decides.
3. No ceiling (turned off by the owner, or only provider limits) is a quiet night. It never writes the
   ceiling and never changes the profile.
